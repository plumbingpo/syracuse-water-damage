Storm Damage Repair Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Ice dams form when attics run warm, so check that yours has enough insulation and airflow
  • Photograph your property once a year so its condition is on record for insurance purposes
  • In areas that see hurricanes, fit storm shutters or windows rated for impact
  • If storm damage breaks a pipe you will need the main shutoff, so find it and practice now
  • Before storm season each year, get the roof inspected and its problem areas repaired
  • Extend downspouts and slope the soil so runoff moves away from the foundation

Why Hire a Professional for Storm Damage Repair?

Because one kind of storm damage feeds another, the work needs restoration professionals with real experience. A roof leak that looks minor from inside may have done extensive hidden damage to insulation, roof decking and wall cavities, and that leads to mold growth and structural deterioration if it is not found and dried. Professional restoration technicians use thermal imaging and moisture meters to trace water paths and identify every affected area.

Tarping and boarding up only work when they are done with the right technique. An improperly installed tarp can create new problems by channeling water or failing under wind load. Our crews are experienced in storm damage mitigation and use the right materials and methods to give reliable temporary protection while permanent repairs are scheduled.

How to Tell You Need Storm Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A basement or ground floor that floods when the drains cannot keep up
  • Water pooling in the attic, around the chimney or near skylights
  • After a weather event, insulation inside walls or overhead feels soaked
  • Downspouts knocked off and gutter runs hanging after high wind
  • Rooms that start to smell stale or moldy following a storm
  • Flashing that is bent, loose or missing at roof edges, valleys and vents
  • Shingles that are gone, cracked or curled up when you look from the yard
