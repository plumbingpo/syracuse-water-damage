AC Leak Water Damage in Syracuse: What to Know

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the System DownSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Bring In Two ProsCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Leave the system off until it is fixed.
Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • Coil icing brought on by a filter that was not changed or a refrigerant charge that is low.
  • Sweating ducts and pipes in a humid attic wetting the drywall under them.
  • More meltwater than the pan can handle after an iced-over coil thaws.
  • Failure of the small pump that lifts condensate to a drain.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Closet Walls and Platform

In a closet, the unit usually rests on a wooden stand built above the return air box. The wood rots and the nearby drywall draws moisture upward, all of it out of sight in back of the unit.

Trim and Baseboards

Trim at floor level, including the closet door frame, wicks up the leak. Swollen fiberboard pieces do not recover and are normally swapped for new ones.

Ceilings Under the Unit

Overflow from a unit in the attic lands on the ceiling drywall. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.

Insulation Around the Unit

Insulation around an attic unit soaks up overflow and holds it against the ceiling. Soaked sections usually come out, since they no longer insulate.

What We Do

How We Handle AC Leak Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging around the air handler, the ceiling or walls next to it, and the flooring outside the closet to find every wet material, not only the stained ones.

2

Extract and Open Up

We extract the water sitting in the carpet, the platform and the pan. Saturated or moldy insulation and drywall are removed, which opens the cavity to dry air.

3

Set Drying Equipment

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, usually 3 to 5, while we track the readings. We place equipment so the HVAC technician can still reach the unit.

4

Sanitize and Rebuild

Since condensate left standing for days is seldom clean, we apply an antimicrobial to the surfaces it touched. New flooring, baseboard, drywall and insulation follow, and fresh paint makes the area match.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.
