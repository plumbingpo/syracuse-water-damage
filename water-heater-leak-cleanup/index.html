Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Syracuse: What to Know

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our Syracuse crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. Garage leaks wet whatever is stored on the floor, along with the wall the garage shares with the house. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

Before We Arrive

  1. Cut the Power or GasElectric units get shut down at the breaker. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Fabric and cardboard absorb water fast, then keep it pressed to the concrete and walls.
Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Connections that were not fully tightened during a repair or a new installation.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
  • A missing expansion tank or a failed thermostat that lets too much pressure build inside.
  • The drain valve at the bottom of the tank cracking, loosening or failing to shut fully.
  • Years of sediment buildup and corrosion wearing a hole in the base of an old steel tank.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Attic and Ceilings

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Wet insulation stops insulating and its weight can bring ceiling drywall down.

Garage Wall Base

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Closet Walls

Water wicks up the drywall around an interior heater closet and into the rooms on the other side. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.

Stored Belongings

Whatever is kept by a closet or garage heater, from keepsakes to furniture and boxes, is the first thing to get wet. Wet contents may grow mold unless they are dried or thrown out soon, and cardboard simply collapses.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Extract the Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check the platform, wall bases, adjoining rooms and any ceiling below to find the full extent of the wet area.

3

Remove Wet Material

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for the 3 to 5 days drying usually takes. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.
