Water Damage to Hardwood Floors in Syracuse, NY

Home / Blog / Water Damage to Hardwood Floors in Syracuse, NY

Hardwood floors are one of the most valuable features in any home, and they are also among the most vulnerable to water damage. Boards that get wet can stain, buckle, crown, or cup, and mold may grow in the subfloor underneath. For Syracuse, NY homeowners with hardwood floors, it is important to understand how to respond to water exposure and when professional restoration can save your floors versus when replacement is necessary.

Why Hardwood Reacts to Water

The term for wood is hygroscopic: it gains or loses moisture depending on its surroundings. Wood fibers expand as they take on moisture, which is what happens when a floor gets too wet. The earliest sign you can see of a moisture imbalance is cupping, where board edges sit higher than the middle. With continued exposure, a board can buckle, lifting completely off the subfloor, or crown, its middle higher than the edges because the upper face dries quicker than the underside. Mold is another risk of long exposure, since it grows unseen between the subfloor and the hardwood and may not be found until the floor comes up.

Is a Wet Hardwood Floor Salvageable?

Saving a wet hardwood floor comes down to the subfloor's condition, the hardwood's type and finish, the length of time it stayed wet, the amount of water, and how soon professional drying starts. In many cases, hardwood floors that receive professional drying within 24 to 48 hours of clean water exposure can be saved. Success depends on drying the wood gradually and under control, which lets it give up moisture evenly instead of cracking and splitting.

Drying Wood Floors the Professional Way

Professional hardwood floor drying uses specialized techniques that are different from standard structural drying. To pull moisture evenly from the wood, mats or panels go directly on the surface and create a controlled drying environment. Another option is to run a floor drying system under the subfloor, reaching it through the crawl space. The key is gradual, even drying, because drying too rapidly makes the surface dry faster than the core and leads to permanent cupping, cracking, and splitting.

Signs the Floor Cannot Be Saved

As a rule, hardwood has to be replaced after more than 48 to 72 hours of being wet, after contact with contaminated water such as floodwater or sewage, when delamination or buckling is widespread, when mold is growing on the subfloor, or when the subfloor has itself been damaged.
